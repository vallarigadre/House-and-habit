For a decade, the safe answer for a living room was greige. Soft, pleasant and, if we are honest, a little forgettable. This fall the color showing up everywhere is warmer, deeper and much more grounded. Chocolate brown is on sofas, velvet pillows, lampshades, kitchen cabinets and even ceilings, and it makes a room feel like a favorite sweater.

This is not just a feeling. In the annual 1stDibs survey of interior designers, chocolate brown was the most cited color for 2026, with a third of designers planning to use it, almost double the share from 2022. Burgundy, its moody cousin, has tripled over the same period.

The good news is that brown is one of the easiest trends to live with. It is a neutral, so it plays nicely with what you already own, and you do not need to repaint a thing to try it.

## Why brown feels right now

Brown reads as natural and calm. It belongs with the materials that already make a home feel lived in: wood, linen, wool, leather, clay and stone. After years of cool gray and stark white, it feels fresh without being loud.

It also does something gray never could. Brown brings its own warmth. A room built around it needs fewer accessories to feel finished, because the color is already doing the cozy work for you. That is exactly what you want as the evenings draw in.

## The shades that work

Not all browns are equal, and the shade you choose changes the mood completely.

**Cocoa** is the easiest place to start. It is medium in depth with a slightly red undertone, and it looks wonderful on upholstery, especially textured fabrics like boucle and velvet.

**Espresso** is deep and dramatic, almost black in low light. Use it in one confident place: a painted bookcase, a velvet armchair or a single accent wall behind the sofa.

**Milk chocolate** sits close to caramel and toffee. It is the gentlest of the three and pairs beautifully with cream, oat and soft white for a light, warm room.

**Cognac** is the color of good leather. A pouf, a leather sling chair or a pair of cushions in cognac adds richness and a slightly vintage feel.

## What to pair it with

Brown loves contrast. These pairings are the ones designers keep returning to.

- **Cream and bone.** The classic. Cream keeps brown light and airy, and the combination looks expensive in any room.
- **Sage and olive.** Earthy and calming, like a walk in the woods. Great with lots of plants.
- **Plum and burgundy.** Moody and romantic, and perfect for the holiday season ahead.
- **Rust and terracotta.** Very seventies and very cozy. Add a little brass and it glows.
- **Pale blue.** The surprising one. Cornflower and powder blue are rising in designer surveys too, and blue with brown is a crisp, elegant contrast.

For metals, brass and aged gold add a soft glow, while black iron keeps the look sharp and modern.

## How to keep brown from feeling heavy

The fear with brown is that it will make a room dark or dated. Three rules keep it fresh.

**Balance dark with light.** For every deep brown piece, add something cream, white or pale. A roughly 60 to 40 split between light and dark tones keeps a room airy.

**Vary the texture.** Brown on brown works beautifully when the materials change: velvet next to leather next to wool next to wood. Flat, matching brown everywhere is what looks dated.

**Let light in.** Keep window treatments sheer during the day, and use plenty of lamps at night. Brown looks its best in warm, low light.
