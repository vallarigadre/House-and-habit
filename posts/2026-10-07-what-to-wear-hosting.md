The host has the hardest outfit brief of the evening. You need to look pulled together when you open the door, then spend the next five hours cooking, pouring, clearing plates, bending into the oven and maybe sitting on the floor for a game of charades. Guests get to dress for the party. You have to dress for the party and the job.

The secret is choosing pieces that look effortless but are secretly practical: fabrics that move, waistbands that forgive a second helping, and sleeves that will not drag through the gravy. The best advice from fashion editors is the same every year: keep it simple and festive, so your attention can go to the food, the table and the music.

Here are five formulas that work for everything from a casual Friendsgiving to a candlelit holiday dinner.

## 1. The knit set

**The formula:** a matching knit top and wide-leg pants in one warm neutral.

A knit set is the hosting uniform of the season. It looks intentional and expensive, it feels like pajamas, and because the pieces match, there is zero thinking involved. Camel, oatmeal, chocolate and soft gray all work beautifully with fall tables.

**Finish it with:** gold hoops, a slick low bun and loafers or ballet flats.

## 2. Satin and something relaxed

**The formula:** a satin or silky button-down with jeans or soft trousers.

There is something about a silky shirt that instantly says "I made an effort," even when the rest of the outfit is denim. Satin catches candlelight beautifully, and a button-down lets you roll the sleeves up past the elbow for cooking.

**The formula:** high-waisted wide-leg trousers with a fitted top.

Wide-leg trousers stay polished all night, never wrinkle at the knee when you sit, and the high waist means a fitted tee, bodysuit or fine knit always looks neat. Choose a fabric with a little stretch, and look for an elastic back waistband if you want to fully enjoy dessert.

**The formula:** a midi sweater dress with flat boots.

One piece, done. A sweater dress is warm, easy and suits every kind of gathering, from a cozy Friendsgiving to a holiday open house. Ribbed knits skim rather than cling, and a midi length works with boots, loafers or flats.

**The formula:** one bold, festive top with simple everything else.

When the menu is simple, let your top do the talking. Velvet is the fabric of the season: it feels holiday without sequins and looks gorgeous in low light. Puff sleeves, a bow or a deep jewel tone like emerald, burgundy or plum all work.

Gold hoops are the one accessory that suits every formula above. They frame your face in photos and never get in the way of cooking the way a bracelet or long necklace can. ## Host-proof details

**Shoes you can stand in.** You will be on your feet for hours. Loafers, ballet flats, low block heels or smart sneakers. Save the stilettos for someone else's party.

**Sleeves that roll.** Avoid long, loose or bell sleeves near the stove. Anything you can push up past the elbow is a winner.

**Forgiving fabrics.** Knits, satin, crepe and anything with stretch. Avoid stiff fabrics that crease when you sit, and anything that shows every splash.

**A pretty apron.** Keep a good apron by the stove for the final stretch of cooking. A [Caldo linen bib apron with pockets](https://link.amazon/B06EXxePs) looks good enough to answer the door in, and the adjustable strap fits everyone.

**Dress early.** Get dressed 30 minutes before guests arrive, not five. You will be calmer, and you will have a moment to check the table, light the candles and pour yourself a drink.
