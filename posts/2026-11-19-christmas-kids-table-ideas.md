Some of the best Christmas magic lives in New York in December: the glowing windows around Rockefeller Center, the skaters under the tree, and the FAO Schwarz flagship, where people of every age line up to dance across the giant floor piano. FAO Schwarz has been a toy store since 1862, and since September 2026 it has an official store on Amazon. That means a little of that New York feeling can now land under your own tree.

It also inspired this post. Holiday dinner with kids goes best when the children have a place that feels made just for them. A good kids Christmas table is not a folding table pushed into a corner. It is a table they can draw on, with their name at their seat, a drink they love, food they will actually eat, and somewhere to go when they are done. Here is exactly how to set one up, piece by piece, with a checklist at the end.

## Where to put the kids' table

Put it close. The kids' table works best within sight of the grown-ups, ideally in the same room or right through an open doorway. Children stay seated longer when they can still see their parents, and you can catch a spilled cup before it travels.

A few practical rules:

- **Size the seats to the kids.** A low activity table or a card table with the legs set short works better than a dining chair with a cushion stacked on it. For a mix of ages, put the youngest at the end nearest a grown-up.
- **Leave elbow room.** Allow about 2 feet per child so crayons and cups do not collide.
- **Keep the floor clear.** Two or three steps of open space between the kids' table and the play corner keeps traffic moving and trips away.
- **Skip the candles.** Use battery tea lights or a string of warm white fairy lights in a jar if you want a glow.

## The kids Christmas table setup, piece by piece

![A kids' Christmas table covered in brown kraft paper with drawn place mats, a mason jar of crayons, red place cards and small soldier teddy bears at each seat](/images/christmas-kids-table-ideas-2.jpg)

### A kraft paper runner they can color on

Roll a [large brown kraft paper roll, 36 inch x 100 feet](https://link.amazon/B09TyE5ro) over the whole table and tape it underneath. At 36 inches wide, it covers a standard card table in one sheet. Before guests arrive, draw a simple plate circle at each seat with a marker, then add a few prompts around the table: "Draw Santa's sleigh", "How many presents can you count?", "Design your own ornament". The paper is the tablecloth, the placemat and the activity, and cleanup is one roll into the recycling bin.

### A crayon cup at every two seats

Fill short jars or cups with [Crayola washable crayons, large size, 8 colors](https://link.amazon/B0e9bJoB7). Large crayons are easier for little hands to hold and harder to snap, and washable matters when someone inevitably draws on a sleeve. One cup between two children is plenty and saves the "she has the red one" moment.

### Place cards and a tiny teddy at each seat

Name cards make kids feel like real guests. Write each name on [Fancy Land Christmas place cards, 36 pack](https://link.amazon/B0cVywmji) and stand one at every seat. Then add the detail they will remember: an [FAO Schwarz 7 inch Soldier Teddy Bear with gift bag](https://link.amazon/B00zMS9D7) sitting beside each card. It doubles as a party favor, a dinner companion and a nod to the toy store that started all of this. Check the age label on the listing before giving any plush to a baby or young toddler.

### Cups with lids and straws

Lids save the evening. Set out [Sliner Christmas cups with lids and straws, 24 pack](https://link.amazon/B02KtMsUg), and write each child's name on a strip of washi tape around the cup so drinks do not get mixed up. Kids love carrying them to the play corner, and a tipped cup stays a non-event.

### Plates, napkins and a tiny centerpiece

Use your own unbreakable plates or sturdy paper plates, and a stack of extra napkins at each end. For a centerpiece, keep it low and touchable: a small tray of clementines and a few pinecones, or a little bowl of candy canes for after dinner. Anything a child cannot reach over safely does not belong here.

## Kid-friendly drinks that feel festive

Give the kids their own drinks station on a tray near their table so they feel grown-up without needing to cross the room.

- **Sparkling apple cider** poured into their lidded cups, with a cinnamon stick for older kids.
- **Cranberry fizz:** half cranberry juice, half sparkling water, with a few frozen cranberries.
- **Candy cane milk:** cold milk with a candy cane to stir, served with dessert.
- **Warm cocoa, not hot.** If you serve hot chocolate, cool it to just warm before it goes in a child's cup.
- **A water pitcher** with a lid, always on the tray.

## A simple kids' Christmas menu

![A Christmas kids' buffet tray with a cucumber and cherry tomato Christmas tree, mini mac and cheese cups, ham and cheese pinwheels and a fruit wreath](/images/christmas-kids-table-ideas-3.jpg)

Kids eat best when the food is small, familiar and easy to pick up. Serve this menu on one tray or a small buffet just for them, while the grown-ups have the main meal.

- **Veggie Christmas tree:** cucumber rounds and halved cherry tomatoes arranged in a triangle on a platter, a yellow pepper star on top, and a small bowl of ranch.
- **Mini mac and cheese cups:** bake your usual mac and cheese in a muffin tin for neat, grab-able portions.
- **Ham and cheese pinwheels:** spread a tortilla with cream cheese, layer ham and cheese, roll tightly and slice into rounds. For a vegetarian version, fill them with hummus and thin cucumber ribbons.
- **Buttered noodles or plain rice** for the pickiest eaters.
- **A fruit wreath:** grapes, strawberries and banana slices arranged in a ring, with a few mint leaves.
- **Dessert:** a sugar cookie each, plus a bowl of whipped cream for dipping.

For a gluten-free option, swap in gluten-free pasta and corn tortillas. For children under 4, cut grapes and cherry tomatoes into quarters lengthwise and keep whole nuts and hard candy off the table.

## The play corner near the table

Kids finish dinner long before the adults do. A play corner a few steps away lets them leave the table happily while you finish your plate in peace.

Choose a corner you can see from the dinner table and roll out a rug or a soft blanket. Then add:

- **A cozy spot.** The [Comfy Cubs kids play tent](https://link.amazon/B053L5h2A) gives them a little room of their own. Fill it with pillows, a throw and a basket of Christmas books.
- **Something to move to.** The [FAO Schwarz Giant Dance Mat Piano](https://link.amazon/B0iRc5ptx) is the at-home version of that famous floor piano. At 101 by 29 inches it fits along a hallway wall or in front of the tree, and after dinner it turns into a family dance-off.
- **Something quiet to build.** A box of blocks keeps older kids busy at a low coffee table.
- **A rule card.** "Shoes off in the tent, voices low after 7" works better than reminders shouted from the dinner table.

Leave the floor between the table and the corner clear, and set a grown-up "check-in" every 20 minutes so nobody is left out.

## Kids Christmas table setup checklist

**The week before**
- Count the kids and their ages, and pick a table and seats sized for them.
- Order place cards, crayons, cups and favors.
- Plan the kids' menu and drinks.

**The day before**
- Set up the table and roll out the kraft paper runner.
- Draw a plate circle and a few coloring prompts at each seat.
- Write the place cards and set a teddy beside each one.
- Label the cups with washi tape.
- Set up the play corner and do a quick safety check of the floor.

**The day of**
- Prep the veggie tree, pinwheels and fruit wreath.
- Bake the mac and cheese cups 30 minutes before dinner.
- Fill the crayon cups and the drinks tray.
- Seat the kids first and serve their food a few minutes before the grown-ups.
- Keep the gifts under the tree until the plates are cleared.
