Friendsgiving is the best holiday of the year for one simple reason: nobody is obligated to be there. It is the dinner where you get to choose the guest list, mix up the menu and set the tone. The only thing that can spoil it is a host who spends the whole evening at the stove with flushed cheeks and a timer going off every four minutes.

The secret is boring but powerful. Do almost everything before the day itself. This guide walks you from three weeks out to the moment the doorbell rings, so that on the night you are holding a drink, not a baster.

Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26 this year, which makes the Saturday before, November 21, the natural Friendsgiving date. Most friends are still in town, and nobody is already sick of turkey.

## Three weeks before: lock the plan

**Pick the date and send the invite.** A group text works fine, but give people a clear time, an address and one line about the vibe: cozy, casual, bring something you love.

**Decide what you are cooking.** As the host, you own the main dish and the gravy. Everything else can be shared. If eight is your number, a 12 to 14 pound turkey is plenty with leftovers. Short on oven space or nerves? A spatchcocked turkey or a bone-in turkey breast roasts in about half the time of a whole bird.

**Set up a potluck sign-up.** A shared spreadsheet or note with categories stops the classic Friendsgiving problem of five people bringing mac and cheese. The categories that work best:

- Two vegetable sides
- Two starchy sides (stuffing, potatoes, rolls)
- One salad
- Two desserts
- Drinks and ice

Encourage friends to bring a dish from their own family tradition. A Friendsgiving table with a grandmother's sweet potato pie next to someone's favorite biryani or tamales is more memorable than a perfect replica of a magazine menu.

## The weekend before: make ahead

Some Thanksgiving staples genuinely improve with time. Get these done and stored:

1. **Cranberry sauce.** Make it up to a week ahead. It thickens and the flavor deepens in the fridge.
2. **Pie dough.** Wrap discs tightly and freeze. Thaw in the fridge the night before you bake.
3. **Turkey stock.** Roast a pack of wings with onion and carrot, then simmer. It is the base for a gravy that tastes like you fussed.
4. **The playlist.** Seriously. Build it now so you are not scrolling with greasy fingers later.

## Two days before: shop and set

**Do the big shop.** Leave only fresh herbs, flowers and bread for the day itself.

**Start thawing a frozen turkey** if you have not already. Plan on about a day in the fridge for every four to five pounds, so a 14 pound bird needs three full days.

**Set the table.** This sounds premature, but it is the single best hosting habit there is. Lay the tablecloth, plates, glasses and napkins. A natural linen cloth, like this [Solino Home 60 x 120 inch linen tablecloth](https://link.amazon/B04eQRfCe), looks relaxed and beautiful even slightly rumpled, which is exactly the Friendsgiving mood. Add [kraft place cards](https://link.amazon/B0eb7JfjZ) and write names while you have a quiet moment.

## The day before: prep like a restaurant

- **Dry brine the turkey.** Pat it dry, rub generously with kosher salt mixed with a little sugar and herbs, and leave it uncovered in the fridge overnight. The skin dries out and roasts up crisp and golden.
- **Chop everything.** Onions, celery, herbs for stuffing, garlic for the green beans. Store in labeled containers.
- **Chill the drinks.** Wine, sparkling water and a big batch of a seasonal cocktail. Cranberry and orange spritzes or a cider punch are easy to make by the pitcher.
- **Make dessert.** Pies are happiest made a day early.

## The day of: the hour-by-hour plan

These timings assume a 14 pound turkey and guests arriving at 5 p.m. Adjust backwards from your own arrival time.

**12:00 p.m.** Take the turkey out of the fridge so it can lose its chill. Pick up flowers and bread.

**1:00 p.m.** Oven to 425°F. Turkey goes in on the rack, then drop the oven to 350°F after the first 30 minutes. An unstuffed 14 pound bird usually needs two and a half to three hours in total, so this start time leaves you a buffer.

**3:00 p.m.** Stuffing goes in alongside, if your oven has room. Start the gravy base with your stock.

**3:30 p.m.** Start checking the thickest part of the thigh. You are looking for 165°F; many cooks pull at 160°F and let the rest carry it the final few degrees.

**4:00 p.m.** Turkey out, loosely tented with foil. It needs at least 30 minutes to rest, and it will stay hot far longer than you expect. Now finish the gravy with the pan drippings. A [fat separator](https://link.amazon/B0cO0L7sp) pours off the grease and leaves you with pure, flavorful juices, which is the difference between a good gravy and a great one.

**4:30 p.m.** Put out a snacking board: nuts, cheese, grapes, crackers. Light candles. Get dressed.

**5:00 p.m.** Guests arrive. Hand everyone a drink and, crucially, a job. One friend carves, one warms sides in the still-hot oven, one runs the music.

**5:45 p.m.** Sit down. Make a toast. Eat.

## The potluck logistics nobody thinks about

**Oven and stovetop space.** Ask guests to bring dishes that arrive ready to serve, or that only need a quick warm through. Ask about this in your sign-up so that nobody shows up needing an hour in the oven.

**Keeping dishes warm in transit.** If you are the guest bringing a hot casserole, an [insulated casserole carrier that fits a 9 x 13 dish](https://link.amazon/B0abhTXrJ) keeps it hot on the drive and stops spills. It also makes a thoughtful host gift.

**Dietary needs.** Ask in the invite. One clearly labeled vegan or gluten-free side means everyone gets a full plate.

**Leftovers.** Stack a pile of takeaway containers by the door. Sending friends home with a little box of turkey and pie is a lovely end to the night.

## A simple table that looks generous

You do not need a florist. Lay a runner of greenery down the middle of the table: eucalyptus, rosemary, a few small white pumpkins and some pears or pomegranates. Add [ivory taper candles](https://link.amazon/B0gW4G4o6) at different heights. Mix your plates if you do not have eight matching ones; alternating two patterns looks completely intentional.

An edible centerpiece of figs, grapes, nuts and a little honey doubles as a snack for anyone hovering before dinner.

## The only rule that matters

Friendsgiving is about the people around the table, not a flawless menu. If the stuffing is a little dry or the rolls are store bought, nobody will remember. They will remember that you lit the candles, poured them a drink and made a toast. Plan ahead, hand out jobs and sit down with your friends.
