Pink Christmas has quietly become one of the most saved holiday looks of the season, and it is easy to see why. Blush pink sits beautifully next to pine green, it glows under fairy lights, and it feels softer and more personal than the usual red and gold.

The trick is keeping it cozy. Too much pink and a kitchen starts to feel like a candy shop. The kitchens that get it right mix blush pink with deep pine green, warm cream, a few red bows and a touch of gold. That is the exact palette behind our Pink Pine collection, and below are six easy ways to use it, from a two-minute oven door swap to a full pink and green Christmas table.

## The pink Christmas palette that always works

Before you buy a single thing, settle on a palette. It keeps everything looking intentional instead of random.

- **Blush pink** as the main color: soft and dusty, never hot pink.
- **Pine green** in two shades, a deep forest green and a soft sage, so the pink has something to play against.
- **Warm cream** as the background, on towels, plates and linens, so the room still feels calm.
- **Red bows** as the accent, used sparingly: on a garland, a napkin or a wreath.
- **Gold** in tiny doses: candlesticks, cutlery, a star on top.

If you only remember one rule, make it this one: pair every pink piece with something green. That is what makes pink look like Christmas.

## 4. Set a pink and green Christmas table

If you are hosting, the table is where pink Christmas really shines. Here is the simple formula:

- **Cream stoneware plates** as the base, so the pink pops.
- **Pink Christmas tree napkins** folded on each plate, tied with thin pink velvet ribbon and tucked with a sprig of pine and a few red berries.
- **Pink taper candles** in brass candlesticks down the middle.
- **A pine garland** running the length of the table, with a few pinecones tucked in.
- **Gold cutlery** if you have it, or plain silver if you do not.

Our Pink Christmas Tree Cloth Napkins come in a set of four, 18 x 18 inches each, in the same pink and green watercolor trees as the tea towel, so your kitchen and table match without trying too hard.

**Styling tip:** keep the centerpiece low. Guests should be able to see each other over the candles and greenery.

## Pink Christmas kitchen checklist

If you want the whole look in one afternoon, here is the order we would do it in:

1. Pick your palette: blush, pine, cream, a little red and gold.
2. Swap the oven towel for a pink Christmas tree tea towel.
3. Stand one statement piece on the counter, like a personalized board.
4. Add a pink bottle brush tree or two and a jar of candy canes.
5. Put out pink mugs by the coffee maker.
6. Set the table with pink napkins, pink tapers and a pine garland.
7. Drape a pink throw on the sofa to carry the look into the living room.
