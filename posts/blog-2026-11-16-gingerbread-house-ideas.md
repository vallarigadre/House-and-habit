# Gingerbread House Ideas: how to host a gingerbread decorating party

*A decorating station that runs itself, 6 house designs with the exact candy for every detail, easy snacks, cocoa for the crowd and a setup checklist.*

*This post contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases, at no extra cost to you.*

There is a moment at every gingerbread party when the room goes quiet. Grown-ups who were chatting a minute ago are hunched over a tiny roof, tongue between teeth, placing one gumdrop at a time. That is the magic of it: a gingerbread decorating party gives everyone something to do with their hands, and the conversation flows around it.

It is also one of the easiest holiday parties to host, as long as you plan the station properly. Here are the gingerbread house ideas that make it work: how to set up the table, six house designs with exactly which candy makes each detail, snacks and drinks that keep sticky fingers happy, and a checklist you can follow the day before and the day of.

## The plan: who, when and how many houses

Keep it to two or three hours on a weekend afternoon, around 2 to 5 pm, when the light is good and nobody is rushing off to dinner. Six to twelve guests is the sweet spot: enough for a happy, busy table, few enough that you are not refilling candy every five minutes.

Decide early whether guests decorate one house each or work in teams. For adults and older kids, one small house each feels special and makes a lovely take-home favor. For a mixed group with little ones, pair a child with a grown-up on one larger house.

The single best decision you can make: **build the houses before anyone arrives**. Assembly is the fiddly, frustrating part, and the icing needs a few hours to set firm. When guests sit down to a standing house, they go straight to the fun part.

### Kits or homemade walls?

Both work. A kit saves you a whole day and comes with pre-cut walls that actually fit together. The [Wilton build-it-yourself mini village kit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B06Y5CHVDG?tag=creativedi03c-20) gives you several small houses from one box, which is ideal for a one-house-per-guest party. For a single showpiece the group decorates together, the [Create A Treat large gingerbread house kit](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B00AG1X1J0?tag=creativedi03c-20) is a sturdy choice.

If you bake your own walls, add a little extra flour to your favorite gingerbread dough so it holds sharp edges, roll it about a quarter inch thick, and cut the shapes before baking rather than after. Bake a day ahead so the pieces are fully cool and crisp.

## The decorating station: set it up like a pro

A good station is what separates a calm party from a sticky scramble. Lay it out in this order along the table so guests can move down it without crossing paths.

### Cover the table

Roll [brown kraft paper](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0788YRV9V?tag=creativedi03c-20) down the full length of the table and tape it underneath. It catches every sprinkle, you can write each guest's name at their seat in marker, and cleanup is one roll and one trip to the recycling bin.

### Give every house a board

Set each house on its own [gold square cake board](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07RRYRBZG?tag=creativedi03c-20). It looks finished, it leaves room for a snowy yard and a candy path, and guests can carry their house home without it cracking. If you have one, a [cake decorating turntable](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B082PZ5C8W?tag=creativedi03c-20) at the head of the table is a treat for whoever takes on the big showpiece house, because they can spin it instead of leaning over it.

### Fill the candy jars

Candy is the centerpiece, so display it like one. Clear [glass apothecary jars](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07L51KXTY?tag=creativedi03c-20) down the middle of the table hold the big items (gumdrops, peppermints, candy canes), while [small ceramic bowls](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H4PSH6S9?tag=creativedi03c-20) at each place hold the small stuff within reach. Plan roughly one jar of each big candy per six guests and one bowl of mixed small candy per two.

### Prepare the icing

Royal icing is the glue and the snow. Make it with [meringue powder](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000ZNOVCA?tag=creativedi03c-20) instead of raw egg whites, so it is safe for everyone and sets hard. You want it thick, about the texture of peanut butter, for building, and slightly looser for piping details.

Fill [disposable piping bags](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09FF41FMF?tag=creativedi03c-20) about halfway, twist the tops, and stand them in tall glasses so they do not tip over. Give each guest two bags: one with a small round tip for lines and dots, one with a star tip for snow drifts and garlands. Keep extra icing covered with a damp towel so it does not crust over.

## 6 gingerbread house ideas (and the candy for every detail)

Put a little card with these designs on the table so guests who freeze in front of a blank roof have somewhere to start.

### 1. The classic candy cottage

The one everyone pictures. Overlapping rows of gumdrops for roof shingles, a peppermint swirl on each gable, candy cane posts at the door, and white piped icing icicles dripping from the eaves. Finish with a [Christmas sprinkles mix](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08NLTTH79?tag=creativedi03c-20) pressed into the icing along the roofline.

### 2. The log cabin

Stick pretzel rods horizontally across the walls with icing for logs, use sliced almonds layered like shingles on the roof, and build a chimney from stacked caramel squares. A path of chocolate chips leads to the door, and a few green-iced sugar cone trees stand in the yard.

### 3. The snowy Alpine chalet

Cover the roof with frosted mini wheat cereal, frosted side up, so it looks like snow-heavy tiles. Pipe thick white icing along every edge, add square pretzel windows with icing panes, and dust the whole board with shredded coconut for fresh snow.

### 4. The Victorian painted lady

For the detail lovers. Pipe scalloped trim along the roofline and window frames, use pastel candy melts as round roof tiles, line the porch with mini candy canes as railings, and add tiny silver dragees as doorknobs and lamp posts.

### 5. The stained glass chapel

Best with homemade walls, since you need to cut window shapes before baking. Fill the openings with crushed hard candies and bake until they melt into glassy panes. Keep the outside simple and white so the colored windows glow, especially with a battery tea light inside.

### 6. The modern minimalist

A favorite with grown-ups who want something chic. Use white icing only, a roof of white nonpareils or coconut, one wreath made of a green-tinted icing swirl and a single red candy, and a sprig of fresh rosemary as a tree by the door. Less candy, more restraint, and it looks beautiful on a mantel all season.

## Snacks and drinks for decorators

Guests will snack on the candy no matter what, so balance it with something savory and filling. Keep food on a separate side table, away from the icing.

### A savory spread

- **Slider bar:** small rolls with ham and swiss or turkey and cranberry, warmed in the oven and wrapped in foil.
- **A big winter salad:** arugula, pears, pomegranate seeds, toasted pecans and a maple vinaigrette, served in one large bowl.
- **Soup in mugs:** a tomato or butternut squash soup kept warm on the stove, ladled into mugs so nobody needs a spoon.
- **Something crunchy:** a bowl of popcorn tossed with melted butter and a pinch of cinnamon.

For gluten-free guests, offer the sliders on lettuce wraps or gluten-free rolls, and check that your candy and decorations are labeled gluten-free. For vegetarians, swap the meat in a few sliders for roasted vegetables and fresh mozzarella.

### Cocoa for the crowd

Hot chocolate is the natural drink of a gingerbread party. Make a big batch in a [6 quart slow cooker](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07V7GQBB5?tag=creativedi03c-20): 8 cups of whole milk, 2 cups of heavy cream, 1 1/2 cups of chopped semisweet chocolate, 1/2 cup of cocoa powder, 1/2 cup of sugar and a splash of vanilla. Cook on low for about 2 hours, whisking now and then, then switch to warm. Set out mini marshmallows, whipped cream and crushed candy canes beside it. For adults, a bottle of peppermint schnapps or Irish cream on the side is a nice touch.

## Your setup checklist

### The week before

- [ ] Order kits, boards, piping bags and meringue powder
- [ ] Buy candy: gumdrops, peppermints, mini candy canes, pretzels, almonds, coconut, sprinkles and sugar cones
- [ ] Send invites with a note to wear clothes that can handle icing

### The day before

- [ ] Bake homemade walls, or open the kits and check every piece
- [ ] Assemble all houses and let the icing set overnight
- [ ] Make the royal icing and store it airtight in the fridge
- [ ] Shop for snacks and chop the salad ingredients

### The day of

- [ ] Morning: roll out the kraft paper and set a board at each place
- [ ] Noon: fill the candy jars and bowls, re-whip the icing and fill the piping bags
- [ ] 1 pm: start the cocoa in the slow cooker and the soup on the stove
- [ ] 1:45 pm: warm the sliders, toss the salad, light a candle and start the Christmas music
- [ ] 2 pm: open the door and let the decorating begin
- [ ] Before guests leave: hand out small boxes or bags so every house gets home in one piece

A gingerbread party is not about perfect houses. It is about the leaning chimney someone's dad insists is intentional, the roof that is more sprinkles than gingerbread, and a table full of people who stayed an hour longer than they planned. Set the station, start the cocoa, and let the mess be part of the fun.
