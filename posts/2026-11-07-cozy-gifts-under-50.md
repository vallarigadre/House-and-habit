## Why "looks expensive" is about texture, not price

Look at any gift guide this season and the same clues repeat: warm amber light, heavy stoneware, plush textures, brass and gold accents, real marble. These are the details our eyes read as quality. A gift that has two of them, say a matte stoneware mug and a brass detail, feels far more considered than a shiny gadget at the same price.

Three quick rules we used for every set below:

- **One warm material per gift.** Amber glass, stoneware, sherpa, marble or brass.
- **A soft, quiet color.** Cream, oat, amber and warm gray always look more luxe than bright primaries.
- **Something to use tonight.** Candles, tea, socks and throws get opened and used the same evening, which is exactly what makes a gift memorable.

## How to wrap any gift so it looks expensive: a checklist

The wrapping is half of the "looks expensive" effect, and it costs almost nothing.

1. **Pick one paper and stick with it.** Plain kraft, matte white or deep green, never glossy prints.
2. **Use real ribbon.** Velvet or silk ribbon in burgundy, cream or forest green, tied in a loose bow with long tails.
3. **Add something natural.** A sprig of cedar, eucalyptus, dried orange slice or a cinnamon stick under the ribbon.
4. **Group small things in a box or basket.** Three items nested together read as a set, not a stack of extras.
5. **Write a real tag.** One line about why you chose it does more than any bow.
