# Christmas Table Decor: 5 easy centerpieces and place settings to copy

*Five complete Christmas table looks, from a weeknight dough bowl to a gold and green dinner, plus a step-by-step for setting the table.*

*This post contains affiliate links. As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases, at no extra cost to you.*

The Christmas table does more work than any other surface in the house this month. It holds the weeknight soup, the cookie decorating, the Saturday dinner with friends and, finally, the big meal itself. So it deserves a plan that is pretty, practical and easy to reset.

Here are five complete Christmas table looks, each with named pieces and exactly what goes where. They run from everyday to dressed up, so you can start simple in early December and build toward the main event. At the end, there is a step-by-step for setting any Christmas table, plus place cards and little favors that make guests feel expected.

## The 3 rules every good Christmas table follows

Before you pick a look, keep three things in mind. Designers repeat them every year because they work.

- **Keep the centerpiece low.** Anything above about 12 inches blocks faces. Tall pieces belong in the form of slim tapers, which you can see past.
- **Build it on a runner or tray.** Grouping the centerpiece on a long runner or board means you can lift it off in one move when the serving dishes arrive.
- **Repeat one material three times.** Brass candlesticks, brass place card holders and gold flatware, for example. Repetition is what makes a table look planned rather than collected.

## Look 1: The everyday December table

This is the table you leave up all month. It survives homework, takeout and a stack of mail, and it still looks like Christmas.

**The centerpiece:** a long wooden dough bowl, such as this [hand-carved paulownia dough bowl](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0B9CLPCFN?tag=creativedi03c-20), filled with clementines, a few pinecones and short sprigs of cedar or pine tucked between the fruit. Eat the clementines during the week and refill on Sunday. That is your whole maintenance plan.

**The light:** a row of three to five [gold mercury glass votives](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B079LV43CV?tag=creativedi03c-20) on either side of the bowl, with tea lights inside. They glow warmly at dinner and look just as good unlit.

**The table:** a plaid or gingham runner down the middle and simple cloth napkins tied in a loose knot at each place. No chargers, no place cards. This is about cozy, not formal.

## Look 2: The heritage tartan table

Heritage style, the preppy, Ralph Lauren kind of Christmas, is one of the strongest looks of the season, and tartan on the table is the fastest way in. It feels traditional without feeling dated.

**The centerpiece:** a red tartan runner topped with a fresh or faux cedar garland running the full length. Nestle a few red apples or pomegranates into the greenery.

**The light:** a cluster of [brass taper candle holders in three heights](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07DR9ZTMR?tag=creativedi03c-20), placed in the center and at each end of the garland, holding [ivory unscented taper candles](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B011ABWSDE?tag=creativedi03c-20). Unscented matters at the table, because candles should never compete with the food.

**The place setting:** a gold charger like these [round gold chargers with a floral rim](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B08DTV47WB?tag=creativedi03c-20), a cream dinner plate, and a plaid napkin pulled through a [pine and pinecone napkin ring](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DD2XDWPY?tag=creativedi03c-20). The rings carry the greenery from the center of the table right to each guest.

## Look 3: The candlelit table with dried oranges

This look borrows from Victorian Christmas: natural materials, lots of candlelight and things that look handmade. It is beautiful for a Christmas Eve supper or a small dinner party.

**The centerpiece:** a [faux eucalyptus garland](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07SHBVF66?tag=creativedi03c-20) laid flat down the table as a living runner, with a [dried orange slice garland with cinnamon sticks](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HF8CQJ7N?tag=creativedi03c-20) woven loosely through it. Add a few whole pomegranates, walnuts in their shells and a handful of fresh cranberries scattered into the gaps.

**The light:** ivory tapers at different heights all along the garland. Mix your brass holders with any vintage candlesticks you already own. Mismatched works here, as long as they share a metal or a color.

**The finishing touch:** a handwritten menu card propped on each plate. Even if the menu is roast chicken and potatoes, writing it out makes dinner feel like an occasion.

![An overhead view of a Christmas table with a eucalyptus garland runner woven with dried orange slices and cinnamon sticks, pomegranates, cranberries and walnuts, ivory taper candles in brass holders and cream plates with handwritten menu cards](https://houseandhabit.blog/images/christmas-table-decor-3.jpg)

## Look 4: The natural Scandinavian table

If red and green feel like too much, go pared back. This look leans on texture instead of color, and it is the easiest to transition into January.

**The centerpiece:** a simple wooden board or tray with three small potted rosemary plants, a few bare birch branches laid flat and a string of wood beads. The rosemary smells wonderful and doubles as favors.

**The place setting:** [round woven rattan chargers](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B016WKOLQU?tag=creativedi03c-20) under white plates, with an [ivory hemstitch linen napkin](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B07FRNTBQ7?tag=creativedi03c-20) folded into a neat rectangle on each plate and a single sprig of rosemary tucked into the fold.

**The light:** white or ivory tapers only, in simple holders. Keep the table bare wood or lay a plain linen cloth. The restraint is the point.

## Look 5: Gold and green for Christmas dinner

This is the dressed-up table, the one for the main meal. It is still easy, because it is built from pieces you have used in the looks above.

**The centerpiece:** the eucalyptus garland down the center again, this time with two [gold hurricane glass candle holders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0HCYYQL4J?tag=creativedi03c-20) holding ivory pillar candles at either end. Fill the space between them with a line of mercury glass votives.

**The place setting:** gold chargers, your best white plates, crystal or simple stemmed glasses and an ivory linen napkin tied with a length of deep green velvet ribbon. Add a small gold ornament to the bow if you want a little sparkle.

**The place cards:** [metal bell place card holders](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B09VK21M2X?tag=creativedi03c-20) above each plate, names handwritten in gold or white pen. Bells feel festive without being cute.

## How to set the Christmas table, step by step

Use this order for any of the looks above. It saves you from moving things twice.

1. **Clear and clean.** Wipe the table and decide whether it will be bare wood, a tablecloth or a runner.
2. **Lay the runner or cloth.** Center it and let it hang evenly at both ends, about 6 to 12 inches over the edge.
3. **Build the centerpiece.** Garland or greenery first, then candles, then fruit or small objects to fill gaps. Step back and check the height from a seated position.
4. **Place the chargers.** Center one at each seat, about an inch from the table edge.
5. **Add plates and napkins.** Dinner plate on the charger, salad plate on top if you are using one, napkin on the plate or to the left.
6. **Set the flatware.** Forks on the left, knife (blade facing in) and spoon on the right, dessert spoon above the plate.
7. **Add glasses.** Water glass above the knife, wine glass just to its right.
8. **Finish with place cards and favors.** Then light the candles about 10 minutes before guests sit down.

## Place cards, menus and little favors

Small details are what guests remember and photograph.

- **Place cards:** dark card stock with names in metallic pen looks expensive and costs almost nothing. Tuck a sprig of greenery behind each one.
- **Menus:** one card per guest, or a single menu on a small easel at the center of the table.
- **Favors:** a single clementine with a name tag, a tiny potted rosemary or a wrapped cookie at each plate. Keep it small and edible or plantable, so nothing ends up in a drawer.
