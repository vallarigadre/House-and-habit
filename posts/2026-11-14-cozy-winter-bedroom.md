There is a moment every winter when the bedroom stops feeling like a bedroom and starts feeling like a refrigerator with a headboard. The sheets are cold, the floor is colder, there is a draft coming from somewhere near the window, and it is dark when you wake up and dark again by five.

The fix is not a whole new room. It is a winter edit: a handful of swaps that make the bed warmer, the window tighter, the floor softer and the light kinder. This post walks through each one with named pieces, so you can do the whole room in a weekend or pick the one that bothers you most.

## Why bedrooms are going full cocoon this winter

Design editors have a name for what we all want in January: the cocoon bedroom. Livingetc and Homes & Gardens have both been writing about bedrooms that feel wrapped and layered, with textured fabrics, warm low light and a quieter palette. Homes & Gardens calls a version of it cozy opulence: soft, rich materials that feel a little indulgent without being fussy.

In real life, that means three things. Layers you can add and remove instead of one heavy comforter. Texture you can feel, like flannel, knit, corduroy and faux sheepskin. And light that comes from lamps, not the ceiling. For this edit we use cream, oatmeal and a soft sage green, with one warm accent of tartan.

## Stop the drafts at the window

A surprising amount of cold comes through the glass. Thermal curtains are the easiest fix, and they double as a way to keep dark mornings dark when you want to sleep in.

The [NICETOWN thermal insulated curtains in Biscotti Beige](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B01KWOY7UM?tag=creativedi03c-20) are a warm neutral that blends with cream bedding. Three tips that make any curtain work harder:

- **Hang them high and wide.** Mount the rod a few inches above the window frame and past each side, so the panels cover the edges where the cold sneaks in.
- **Let them touch the sill or the floor.** Gaps at the bottom let cold air pour into the room.
- **Open them on sunny days.** Winter sun is free heat. Close them at dusk to hold it in.

## Light for dark mornings and early evenings

Winter light is the hardest part of the season. The fix is two kinds of light: something gentle to wake up to, and something warm and low for the evening.

### Wake up to a sunrise, even in the dark

A sunrise alarm clock slowly brightens before your alarm time, so you wake to light instead of a buzz in the dark. The [Hatch Restore 2 sunrise alarm clock and sound machine in Putty](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0C5S7K1JK?tag=creativedi03c-20) also plays sleep sounds and works as a dimmable reading light, and the putty color looks like a piece of decor rather than a gadget on the nightstand.

### Turn off the overhead light after dark

In the evening, use only lamps: one on each nightstand, set lower than eye level, with warm white bulbs. Add a candle on the dresser while you get ready for bed, and blow it out before you turn in. ## Your winter bedroom checklist

Do it in one weekend, in this order:

1. **Swap the sheets** for flannel.
2. **Add the heated blanket** between the top sheet and the comforter.
3. **Fluff the comforter** and fold a knit throw across the foot.
4. **Hang thermal curtains** high and wide.
5. **Lay a soft rug** where your feet land.
6. **Set up the lamps** and a sunrise alarm on the nightstand.
7. **Start the humidifier** when the heating comes on.
8. **Bag up summer bedding** and slide it under the bed.
